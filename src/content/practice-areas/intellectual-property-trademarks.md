Trade mark work is unusually date-sensitive. Priority runs from filing, replies to
examination reports must be made within a fixed window, and opposition periods do
not extend because a business was busy. A great deal of what follows depends on
what was done, and recorded, at the beginning.

The work here covers the full life of a mark — checking whether a name is
available, filing and prosecuting the application, responding when the Registry
raises objections, defending or bringing an opposition, and dealing with third
parties who adopt something too close once the brand is established.

Advice is given on the basis of what the register and the evidence actually show.
Where a proposed mark is weak, or where a conflict makes a filing unlikely to
succeed, that is said before the application is made rather than after.
