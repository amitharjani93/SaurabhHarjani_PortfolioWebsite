Civil disputes are decided on records, not on grievances. Two parties usually
agree on far more of the history than they expect; what separates them is what
the documents say about it, and what can be proved.

The work in this area runs from advisory and drafting — where the aim is to keep
a matter from becoming a dispute at all — through notices and negotiation, to
conduct of proceedings where they cannot be avoided.

You will be told at the outset how strong the position appears to be, including
where it is weak, and what pursuing it is likely to involve in time and cost. No
view is offered on the likely outcome of proceedings, because no one is in a
position to give one honestly.
